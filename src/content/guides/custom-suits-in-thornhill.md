Sam's studio sits in Vaughan, right on the Thornhill line, and has for thirty-plus years. If you live in Thornhill and want a real custom suit — measured by hand, cut to you — your neighbourhood tailor is a five-minute drive, not a trip downtown. Here's what that actually gets you.

## Where is Sam's, and how close is it to Thornhill?

Sam's is in Vaughan, sitting right on the Thornhill line — for most of Thornhill it's a short local drive, no 407, no downtown parking.

Thornhill straddles the Vaughan and Markham border along Yonge and Bathurst, and Sam's studio sits inside that catchment. If you're near Bathurst and Centre, Promenade, or Old Thornhill, you're minutes away. And if even that's too far, [Sam comes to you](/guides/the-traveling-tailor) — home or office, anywhere in the GTA.

## Why go to a local tailor instead of a downtown showroom?

Because a suit isn't a one-visit purchase, and every visit is easier when it's around the corner. A custom suit means a fitting, a first try-on, and usually one more pass to get it perfect.

Do that downtown and each step is an afternoon: the drive, the parking, the traffic home. Do it in your own neighbourhood and each step is an hour. Same suit, a fraction of the friction. That's the whole case for using the tailor down the road — [see how long the process takes](/guides/how-long-does-a-custom-suit-take).

## What does Sam actually make?

Full custom suiting and separates, cut to your measurements — business, wedding, formal, and everyday.

- **[Business suits](/business)** — the navy and grey workhorses that photograph well and last years, not seasons.
- **[Wedding suits and tuxedos](/wedding)** — for grooms, fathers, and whole wedding parties.
- **[Traditional and community wardrobe](/traditional)** — the sober, correctly-cut pieces Thornhill's families actually wear, from a tailor who knows the room.
- **Shirts, trousers, and separates** when you don't need the full suit.

Everything starts with [the cloth](/fabrics) — around two hundred fabrics in the book — and [your own design](/design), drawn with you, not picked from a rack.

## Does Sam understand the Thornhill community?

Yes — after three decades on this line, Sam has dressed a lot of the neighbourhood, and the traditional community in particular.

That matters more than it sounds. A tailor who knows the community knows what "modest and correct" means without a lecture, knows the difference between a shul suit and a simcha suit, and knows how to cut something that reads as dignified rather than flashy. If you want the full picture, read [the traditional Jewish wardrobe](/guides/the-traditional-jewish-wardrobe). It's the kind of quiet fluency you only get from a tailor who's part of the neighbourhood, not visiting it.

## What's the convenience really worth for a Thornhill wedding?

For a wedding, local means Sam can measure your whole party in one trip and keep everyone on the same timeline.

Groomsmen scattered across Thornhill, Richmond Hill, and North York? Sam can fit them at one family home in an afternoon, or they can each swing by the studio between errands — nobody's booking a downtown day off. The father of the groom, the groom, the brothers: one tailor, one cloth story, one calendar. [Coordinating groomsmen](/guides/coordinating-groomsmen) walks through how that's kept simple, and if hosting is easier, [the traveling tailor](/guides/the-traveling-tailor) brings the whole fitting to your door.

## What happens at the first visit?

A real conversation, twenty-six measurements by hand, and cloth laid out in the light — no pressure, no deposit to walk in the door.

Sam wants to know how you'll actually wear the suit before a tape ever comes out: the office, the wedding, the weekly wear. Then measurements, then cloth. You leave knowing exactly what you're getting and what it costs. That's it — the first fitting is free, and there's no hard sell waiting on the other side of it.
